# Why SpaceX Will Eat Every Defense Contractor's Lunch

I've been thinking about Chamath's SpaceX valuation thesis, and here's what really hits me: we're watching the death of the military-industrial complex in real time.

The traditional defense contractors — Lockheed, Boeing, Raytheon — have gotten fat and lazy on cost-plus contracts. They literally get paid more when projects go over budget and behind schedule. It's the most perverse incentive structure imaginable, and it's created a culture of bureaucratic mediocrity.

Then SpaceX shows up with their "move fast and break things" mentality. They're launching rockets for a fraction of the cost, iterating at startup speed, and treating space like a software problem to be solved rather than a government program to be milked.

But here's where it gets really interesting: SpaceX isn't just disrupting launch. They're building the infrastructure for 21st-century warfare. Starlink isn't just internet for your RV — it's a military communications backbone that can't be taken down by conventional means. When Ukraine started using Starlink to coordinate drone strikes, the Pentagon probably had an "oh shit" moment.

The old guard spent decades building systems designed to fight the last war. SpaceX is building systems for the next one. Satellite constellations, rapid deployment, real-time global communications, precision manufacturing at scale — this is what modern conflict looks like.

What bugs me is how long it took the market to see this. Defense spending isn't going anywhere. If anything, it's accelerating. But instead of flowing through sclerotic contractors who take 10 years to deliver anything, it's going to companies that can move at commercial speed.

The $2 trillion valuation isn't crazy when you realize SpaceX is positioning itself as the AWS of space-based infrastructure. Every military, every intelligence agency, every country that wants to project power will need what they're building.

The real kicker? Unlike traditional defense contractors who are beholden to one customer (the US government), SpaceX is building dual-use technology with global commercial applications. That's not just a better business model — it's a better innovation model.

We're going to look back at this moment as when the defense industry finally got disrupted by someone who understood that the physics of business matter as much as the physics of rockets.
